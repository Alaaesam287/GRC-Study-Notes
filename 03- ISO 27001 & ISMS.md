## 1. Big Picture

**ISO/IEC 27001 = requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS).**

An ISMS is more than policies and technical controls. It connects:

**Context → Leadership → Risk → Treatment → Controls → Operation → Evidence → Evaluation → Audit → Management Review → Improvement**

**ISO 27001:** ISMS requirements + certification basis  
**ISO 27002:** guidance for information-security controls

---
# 2. ISO 27001 Clauses 4:10

## Clause 4: Context of the Organization

### 4.1 Understanding the organization and its context

Identify **internal and external issues** relevant to the organization's ability to achieve its information-security objectives.

**External:** laws/regulations, threats, customers, suppliers, technology, market  
**Internal:** structure, resources, security maturity, processes, culture, responsibilities

### 4.2 Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties

Identify relevant **interested parties** and their requirements.

Examples:
- Customers → protection of their information
- Employees → protection of personal information
- Regulators → legal/regulatory compliance
- Suppliers → contractual/security requirements

### 4.3 Determining the scope of the ISMS

Define the **boundaries and applicability** of the ISMS.

Consider:  
**activities/services + people + processes + information + technology + relevant dependencies**

### 4.4 Information Security Management System

Establish, implement, maintain, and continually improve the ISMS.

**Remember:**  
Asset = something of value that needs protection  
Interested party = party with relevant relationship/requirements  
Scope = boundaries of the ISMS

---
## Clause 5: Leadership

### 5.1 Leadership and commitment

Top management must demonstrate commitment by:

- integrating the ISMS into business processes
- providing resources
- supporting security objectives
- communicating the importance of security
- supporting continual improvement

### 5.2 Policy

Establish an information-security policy that provides management direction and commitment.

**Policy = what/why**  
**Procedure = how**

### 5.3 Organizational roles, responsibilities and authorities

Security responsibilities and authorities must be assigned and communicated.

**Risk owner:** accountable for the business risk  
**Control owner:** responsible for operating/managing the control  
**GRC:** coordinates, tracks, governs, reports and supports -> does not automatically own every risk/control.

---
# Clause 6: Planning

## 6.1 Actions to address risks and opportunities

Establish a defined risk-management approach.

**Risk process:**  
**Identify → Analyze → Evaluate → Treat → Reassess**

Risk treatment options:
- Avoid
- Reduce/modify
- Transfer/share
- Accept

**Risk treatment ≠ control**

Treatment = decision/action for dealing with risk  
Control = measure used to modify/manage risk

Risk assessment methodology should define things such as:
- likelihood/impact criteria
- risk acceptance criteria
- assessment method
- when reassessment occurs

## 6.2 Information security objectives and planning to achieve them

Objectives should be **measurable**, with appropriate:
- target
- owner
- resources
- timeframe
- measurement method

Example:
> Achieve at least 95% remediation of critical vulnerabilities within the defined SLA.

**Risk = potential problem**  
**Objective = desired measurable outcome**

---
# Clause 7: Support

### 7.1 Resources

Provide the people, budget, technology, tools, time and expertise required for the ISMS.

### 7.2 Competence

Ensure people performing security-related work are competent through appropriate education, training, skills or experience.

**Competence = can perform the job**

### 7.3 Awareness

People understand:
- the security policy
- their responsibilities
- their contribution to the ISMS
- consequences of nonconformity

**Awareness = understand their role**

### 7.4 Communication

Define:  
**what + when + to whom + how** information is communicated.

### 7.5 Documented Information

Control important ISMS information and records.

Examples:
- policies/procedures
- risk methodology/register
- treatment plans
- SoA
- audit results
- training records
- management-review records
- evidence

**Maintain = keep current**  
**Retain = preserve records/evidence**

> A policy saying a control exists does not prove the control operates.

---
# Clause 8: Operation

### 8.1 Operational planning and control

Actually perform and control the processes planned under the ISMS.

**Question:** Are we doing what we planned?

### 8.2 Information security risk assessment

Perform risk assessments at planned intervals and when significant changes occur.

### 8.3 Information security risk treatment

Implement the planned risk-treatment actions and controls.

**Important:** Implementation does not automatically mean effectiveness.

Example:

> Vulnerability patched → rescan/validate → confirm vulnerability is actually resolved.

---
# Clause 9: Performance Evaluation

### 9.1 Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation

Determine:
- what to monitor/measure
- how
- when
- who is responsible

Then analyze and evaluate results.

Example:  
**Awareness target = 95% → actual = 91% → investigate → corrective action.**

### 9.2 Internal Audit

Conduct internal audits at planned intervals to determine whether the ISMS:
- conforms to ISO 27001
- conforms to the organization's own requirements
- is effectively implemented and maintained

Audit planning considers:
- importance of processes
- previous audit results
- changes
- scope/criteria
- auditor objectivity

**Control testing:** Does this control work?  
**Internal audit:** Does this part of the ISMS conform and work effectively?

### 9.3 Management Review

Top management reviews the ISMS to evaluate its:
- suitability
- adequacy
- effectiveness
- alignment with business needs

Inputs can include:
- audit results
- security objectives/performance
- risks and treatment
- incidents
- nonconformities/corrective actions
- changes
- interested-party requirements
- resources
- improvement opportunities

Outputs = **management decisions and actions.**

**Management Review = Evaluate + Decide**

---
# Clause 10: Improvement

### 10.1 Continual Improvement

Continually improve the **suitability, adequacy and effectiveness** of the ISMS.

Improvement doesn't always mean adding controls. It can mean improving:
- processes
- risk assessment
- controls
- automation
- awareness
- resources
- monitoring

### 10.2 Nonconformity and Corrective Action

When a nonconformity occurs:

**React/fix → Determine cause → Check whether similar issues exist → Corrective Action → Review effectiveness → Retain evidence**

**Correction:** fixes the immediate problem.
**Corrective action:** addresses the cause to prevent recurrence.

Example:

> Late awareness training (nonconformity) → hold the missed training = **correction**  
> Find out why scheduling repeatedly fails + implement automated scheduling = **corrective action**

# 3. Annex A

ISO 27001:2022 Annex A contains **93 reference controls** across four themes:

- **A.5 Organizational controls -> 37**
- **A.6 People controls -> 8**
- **A.7 Physical controls -> 14**
- **A.8 Technological controls -> 34**

Annex A is **not a checklist where every control must automatically be implemented.**
**Risk → Treatment → Applicable Controls**

Control selection should consider:
- risk
- treatment decisions
- organizational context
- legal/contractual requirements
- business needs

---
# 4. Statement of Applicability (SoA)

The SoA documents:
- which Annex A controls are applicable
- which are not applicable
- justification for inclusion/exclusion
- implementation status

**Risk Register:**

> What risks exist, who owns them, and how are they being treated?

**SoA:**

> Which controls apply, why, and what is their implementation status?

Example:

**Risk:** Former employees retain access  
→ **Treatment:** Reduce  
→ **Controls:** Access-rights management/offboarding  
→ **SoA:** Applicable + implementation status

> **"Implemented" requires appropriate evidence, not just a policy.**

---
# 5. Control Lifecycle

When evaluating a control, ask:

### 1. Design
Is the control appropriately designed to address the risk?

### 2. Implementation
Has it actually been put in place?

### 3. Operation
Is it being performed as required?

### 4. Effectiveness
Does it consistently achieve its intended purpose?

### 5. Evidence
Can the organization demonstrate all of this?

**Design → Implement → Operate → Test → Evidence → Evaluate**

Example:
**Risk:** Former employees retain payroll access  
**Treatment:** Reduce  
**Control:** Access review/offboarding  
**Evidence:** Review records, approvals, IAM logs, remediation tickets  
**Testing:** Sample accounts and verify review/removal

---
# 6. Control Testing

Basic flow:
**Risk → Control → Test → Evidence → Result → Finding**

Evidence can include:
- logs
- reports
- tickets
- approvals
- configurations
- review records
- test/rescan results

Testing may use sampling. ISO does not prescribe one universal sample size.

Possible conclusions depend on the organization's methodology:

**Effective / Partially Effective / Ineffective / Not Implemented**

> Evidence should demonstrate what actually happened, not just what the policy says should happen.

---
# 7. Internal Audit

Internal audit evaluates whether the ISMS/process:
**Conforms → Is implemented → Is maintained → Is effective**

Typical finding structure:
**Requirement → Condition/Evidence → Result**

Example:
**Requirement:** Disable terminated employees within 24 hours.  
**Condition:** 1/10 sampled accounts was disabled after 4 days.  
**Result:** The termination-access process was not consistently performed within the required timeframe.

Don't claim something occurred unless the evidence demonstrates it.

Follow-up:
**Finding → Root Cause → Corrective Action → Implementation → Effectiveness → Closure**

---
# 8. ISO 27001 Certification

Typical lifecycle:
**ISMS implementation → Stage 1 → Stage 2 → Certification → Surveillance → Recertification**

### Stage 1

Focuses on readiness, including:
- context/scope
- risk methodology
- risk assessment
- treatment
- SoA
- documented information

**Question:** Is the ISMS ready for the certification audit?

### Stage 2

Focuses more deeply on:
- actual implementation
- operation
- effectiveness
- objective evidence

**Question:** Does the ISMS actually work?

---
# 9. GRC Mental Model

When a security issue is discovered:

**Issue → Risk Statement → Likelihood/Impact → Inherent Risk → Risk Owner → Treatment → Controls → Implementation → Evidence → Testing → Residual Risk → Acceptance/Further Treatment → Monitoring → Reporting → Corrective Action/Improvement**
